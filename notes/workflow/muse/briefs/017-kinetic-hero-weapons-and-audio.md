One coherent combat-feel pass: make the six heroes mechanically and visibly different from the first fight; quicker traversal and satisfying attack/reward rhythm; original weapon and combat music/sound assets; actual gameplay screenshot evidence. The director chooses the design and judges it; you implement all deliverables.

## Why, and what good feels like

The current heroes differ in speed/health/opening relics and projectile decoration, but all run the same automatic slash/arrow/missile foundation. Merely recoloring those shots is not sufficient. Sword positioning, twin-blade rhythm, rifle lines, close shotgun bursts, heavy cannon splash and orbiting scythe sweeps must be recognizable without a label. Keep Lantern Hollow's night-fantasy and cute pixel style: moon-metal, lantern cartridges and signal weapons, not generic realistic guns. Strong hits should feel crisp; growth should feel like an earned surge, with readable enemies and no nonstop audio wall.

## Do

1. Build six real primary weapons on the real arena path. Warden: decisive wide sword cuts, a bright crescent and close/mid positioning. Dancer: quick alternating twin blades and directional double cuts. Sage: precise long rifle bolts with a piercing line and restrained muzzle snap. Keeper: short lantern-shotgun fan with a broad close-range burst. Knight: slower heavy cannon shell with a clear blast, recoil and splash. Eclipse: orbiting scythe that sweeps a ring in pulses and rewards weaving nearby. Distinguish cadence, reach, target coverage and damage application, not just paint. Retain sensible core/relic growth and every existing skill's effect; shared auxiliary powers may remain, but primary identity must still read at higher levels. Do not accidentally retain identical generic melee on all ranged heroes or add a second free full-strength weapon to every kit.
2. Raise common traversal speed about 15–20% (112 px/s is the default choice), sharpen start/stop, and shorten the base dash cooldown about 15–20% (0.95 s default). Keep hero movement differences. Tune attack timings for a lively baseline with visible anticipation/impact and bounded effects; do not speed the whole engine, enemy windups, story typing or modal timers. Maintain projectile/node budgets. Damage may be retuned to support the requested faster combat, with actual comparison numbers, not a paid-hero power ladder.
3. Make silhouettes and feedback sell the weapons: carried weapon/attack prop, muzzle/cut/blast/orbit shapes, restrained recoil and hit sparks, short kill/level/core reward accents. Original deterministic pixel assets or a bounded drawn weapon layer are suitable; preserve four directions, foot plant and stable sprite baseline. If regenerating hero sheets, provide deterministic source and current manifest rows; no downloaded source packs. Clarify hero/weapon descriptions and beacon choices in all five languages. Keep story discoveries and enemy warnings readable.
4. Compose original kinetic arena and guardian music loops plus six distinct weapon sounds, impact/kill accents and growth/core/overcharge reward cues. A melodic night motif with driving drums/bass should fit the world; guardian music should escalate rather than simply get louder. Use deterministic offline generators (the existing original WAV generator is a reference), limited polyphony, priority/rate limiting for rapid hits/pickups, mixing headroom, Settings buses and proper scene cleanup. Keep title/story space calm; duck busy combat sounds where dialogue/choices need attention. No copyrighted music, network asset fetch or invasive new dependency. Music must loop without a click and must not restart on every hit. Do not turn every projectile into an AudioStreamPlayer node.
5. Deliver registered behavioral regression checks, accurate reference docs/build notes/asset manifest, and a deterministic desktop capture harness. Capture each of six heroes firing their actual live weapon in a real arena/terrain at early and stronger builds, plus the improved beacon options in five locales. Include short motion evidence and a reproducible audio audition command/file. The harness must call production attacks, observe hits/live effects, wait for appropriate settled UI, and never fabricate attacks by only drawing posed props.
